## What the project is

Twinfang is a Devil May Cry-style character-action prototype in the browser: Three.js r160 from a CDN, one built file `twinfang.html`, authored as parts in `src/` and concatenated by `python build.py`. Models, textures, animation and most sound effects are generated by code at load. The music and some effect layers are short CC0 recordings (`src/samples/`, credits in `src/samples/CREDITS.md`) that `build.py` embeds in the file.

Scope: a corridor with two fights, the Gatewarden boss in a hall, a winding stair, three sealed rooms, the Stormvane boss on the roof. Three weapon sets (Twin Fangs, Blade & Pistol, Great Maul), four enemy types (Husk, Brute, Stalker, Lantern).

The owner designs and playtests; Claude writes the code. They prefer a plain summary of what changed and what was not checked over a description of effort. Their reference images for the characters are in `references/` (git-ignored).

## The review loop and its scores

Each round, two independent critic agents scored a fresh picture set from 1 to 10 against the references and commercial character-action games, then one engineer agent per track fixed the findings in its own worktree. Scores are means over six or seven dimensions; the same scale was held across rounds.

## How it was verified, and the limits of that

On this PC, real GPU, final build:
- `s_bot.mjs`: the bot completes all seven encounters with zero errors. It refills its own health and drains boss health on a timer, so this proves the game does not break, not that it is fair or fun.
- `s_nan.mjs` and `s_ctx.mjs`: zero invalid pixels in every view.
- `s_perf.mjs` at 1920x1080: 3.6 to 5.6 ms per frame on average in quiet scenes. Timed while the bot really fights (a throwaway scenario, about 9,400 frames over four encounters): 4.7 to 5.8 ms average, 99% of frames under 11 ms, five frames over 16.6 ms. The longest was 52 ms, as the first fight's six Husks are built.
- `s_grip.mjs`: 13 borderline lines, all under 0.75 rad per step, none new.
- `s_audio.mjs` passes. Music balance was measured per octave band against a rough metal target (`audio_bands.py`).

Never verified:
- **How anything sounds.** Measured, never heard. Whether the music now feels like the games the owner named is theirs to judge.
- **Motion.** Every effect, the hero's fins and tails, the rank-up flash and all CSS animations were judged from stills.
- **Combat feel and difficulty.** Hit-stop was lengthened and a finisher now opens with dark frames; nothing else in timing or damage was touched, and none of it was played by a person.
- **Aspect ratios other than 16:9**, `?q=low`, and the owner's own browser (Brave).

## Open problems, in priority order

1. **Owner feedback**: the music, the new look of the hero, and the feel of hits and finishers in motion.
2. **Characters are still short of the references** (final critic: "a solid indie cast, not yet the bar"). In its order: the hero's front (the faceplate under the visor is a dark block on a tall neck, the pectorals are two plain white faces, the upper arms are thin rods); the Twin Fangs (dark streaked flats, coil-spring grips, a thin dark bar from the chase camera); the Stormvane's wings (rows of equal boxes, identical square feathers); surfaces that are clean and even with no grime in the recesses; the Stalker, which is still a thin scribble in a fight. Two smaller points to weigh with the owner: the Husk's paint has drifted pale, and the Lantern's ceramic and cyan now sit close to the hero's white and cyan.
3. **Effects** (final critic). The contact point of a hit is still thin white needles with little heat or mass, weakest on bosses; the sword's trail is a flat blue slab and the Maul's a near-closed hoop; the burst after a finisher's cut line is a dark, empty frame; the hero can be lost behind a boss or under an armour break; the style block's dark plate covers the right side of the fight; skill call-outs arrive clipped in the objective slot.
4. **Animation pass.** Player and boss clips are still the old keyframes.
5. The Stormvane's core-charge moment was never photographed: the bot does not trigger it.

## Working rules learned the hard way

- **Bump `BUILD` in `src/01-util.js` with every delivered change**, and tell the owner which number to look for.
- **Rebuild after every edit** (`python build.py`). The owner opens `twinfang.html`, not `src/`.
- **Clamp before `pow`, `sqrt`, `normalize` and division** in every shader. See the shader rule in `README.md`.
- **`fx.rim` is the only thing that drives a character's hit flash, wind-up glow and heat.** Do not also scale emissive in the rig or a boss file; two agents did both in round 1 and the result fought itself.
- **A new foe, or a new material on one, must go through the warm-up block in `14-game.js`** (`G.warm`), or its first appearance stalls the game. `s_perf.mjs` only measures quiet scenes and will not show this; time frames while the bot fights.
- **Do not style by position in the page** (`#title>div:last-child` hid more than it meant to). Give the element an id.
- **`C` is the controls panel.** Do not bind it to anything else.
- **A `//` comment added in the middle of a long line swallows the rest of the line.** Lines in this code are long; put comments at the end.
- **After a scripted edit, check the file's structure.** `grep -c "  apply(dt){" src/07-rig.js` must print 1.
- **Worktrees for agents are cut from `main`.** Tell each agent to `git reset --hard <current commit>` first.
- **Do not change `AREAS`, hit timings or damage numbers casually.** They were tuned by play before this rebuild.
- **No bells.** Any new sound must pass `audio_view.py` with `ring` under about 60 ms.
- Line endings: the repository stores LF; the Windows checkout has CRLF (`core.autocrlf`). `build.py` reads either and writes LF.
